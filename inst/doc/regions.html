Matching within a region

This article shows how to make fuzzy matching prefer the species recorded where the data were collected, by naming a region or giving coordinates. Plant ranges come from the World Checklist of Vascular Plants (WCVP, Govaerts et al. 2021) on the regions of the World Geographical Scheme for Recording Plant Distributions (WGSRPD, Brummitt 2001). Marine ranges come from the distribution records of the World Register of Marine Species (WoRMS), grouped into the Marine Ecoregions of the World (MEOW, Spalding et al. 2007). Installation is covered in Get started.

  1. Declare the region by name or code with region.

  2. Locate the records by coordinates with coords.

  3. Narrow to native or introduced records with range.

  4. Look up region names and codes with taxify_regions().

library(taxify)

A misspelling with two candidates

A misspelt name can be close to two species of different continents. Cirsium acule is a misspelling of Cirsium acaule, the European stemless thistle, which WCVP lists as Cirsium acaulon. The misspelling is closer to Cirsium edule, a North American thistle:

thistle <- c("Cirsium acule", "Gentiana acaulis", "Primula elatour")
res <- taxify(thistle, backbone = "wcvp", verbose = FALSE)
res[, c("input_name", "matched_name", "accepted_name", "fuzzy_dist")]
#>         input_name     matched_name    accepted_name fuzzy_dist
#> 1    Cirsium acule    Cirsium edule    Cirsium edule  0.1538462
#> 2 Gentiana acaulis Gentiana acaulis Gentiana acaulis         NA
#> 3  Primula elatour  Primula elatior  Primula elatior  0.1333333
#> Sources: WCVP 2026.08 | cite() for full citations

With region = "Europe", fuzzy matching prefers the candidates WCVP records in Europe:

eu <- taxify(thistle, backbone = "wcvp", region = "Europe", verbose = FALSE)
eu[, c("input_name", "matched_name", "accepted_name", "fuzzy_dist")]
#>         input_name     matched_name    accepted_name fuzzy_dist
#> 1    Cirsium acule  Cirsium acaulon  Cirsium acaulon  0.2000000
#> 2 Gentiana acaulis Gentiana acaulis Gentiana acaulis         NA
#> 3  Primula elatour  Primula elatior  Primula elatior  0.1333333
#> Sources: WCVP 2026.08 | cite() for full citations

The thistle now matches Cirsium acaulon, at a larger distance that is still within the threshold. The other two rows stay as they were: Gentiana acaulis matches exactly, and exact matches are never filtered; Primula elatour matches the European Primula elatior in both runs.

How the region filter decides

When a region changes a match, as it did for the thistle, one rule decides it. For each name, the fuzzy candidates are looked up in the range data of the region, and a candidate outside the region is dropped when another candidate is inside it. Three rules follow:

  • Exact matches, including those with capitals ignored, are never filtered.

  • A candidate with no range data is kept.

  • When every candidate of a name lies outside the region, all are kept.

WCVP covers vascular plants and WoRMS marine species; a name outside both is matched as without a region, so a list of several groups can carry a region.

Naming the region

A region name is matched regardless of capitals and accents at any of the three WGSRPD levels: a continent ("Europe"), a region within it ("Middle Europe") or a country or smaller unit ("Belgium"). Several regions are combined. taxify_regions() lists the names and codes, searching all three levels:

taxify_regions("Belgium", scheme = "wgsrpd")
#>   code    name   level2_name level1_name scheme
#> 1  BGM Belgium Middle Europe      EUROPE wgsrpd

A code is read directly, so region = "BGM" and region = "Belgium" are the same region. A name or code that matches no region is dropped with a warning, and the call runs without it:

x <- taxify("Cirsium acule", backbone = "wcvp", region = "GRE",
            verbose = FALSE)
#> Warning: Unrecognized region(s) dropped: GRE. See taxify_regions() for valid
#> codes and names.

Greece is GRC, not GRE. With no search term, taxify_regions() returns every region region accepts; the table counts the country-level regions in each continent:

wgsrpd <- taxify_regions(scheme = "wgsrpd")
table(wgsrpd$level1_name)
#> 
#>           AFRICA        ANTARCTIC   ASIA-TEMPERATE    ASIA-TROPICAL 
#>               71               12               52               31 
#>      AUSTRALASIA           EUROPE NORTHERN AMERICA          PACIFIC 
#>               13               41               73               28 
#> SOUTHERN AMERICA 
#>               48

The same codes appear in the native range that add_wcvp() attaches.

Coordinates

coords gives the place as longitude and latitude, in that order. Each point is placed in its WGSRPD region:

brussels <- taxify(thistle, backbone = "wcvp", coords = c(4.35, 50.85),
                   verbose = FALSE)
brussels[, c("input_name", "matched_name")]
#>         input_name     matched_name
#> 1    Cirsium acule  Cirsium acaulon
#> 2 Gentiana acaulis Gentiana acaulis
#> 3  Primula elatour  Primula elatior
#> Sources: WCVP 2026.08 | cite() for full citations

The Brussels point lies in Belgium, so the thistle resolves to Cirsium acaulon, as it did with region = "Europe". A two-column matrix or data frame of points works too, as do an sf object and a terra SpatVector, which are reprojected to longitude and latitude. Points and a region name can be combined. The WGSRPD boundary polygons that place a point are downloaded once. The point-in-polygon test uses terra or sf when one is installed and its own code otherwise; options(taxify.pip_engine = "terra"), "sf" or "native" chooses.

Native or introduced records

By default a species counts as present in a region with any WCVP record there, native or introduced. range = "native" counts only native records, so a species introduced to the region does not take precedence over a native candidate elsewhere. range = "introduced" counts only introduced records, for work on invasions. range has no effect without a region.

taxify(thistle, backbone = "wcvp", region = "Europe", range = "native")

Marine regions

Marine names use the marine_distribution dataset, downloaded on first use. From then on region also takes MEOW ecoregion, province and realm names and codes, and a province or realm stands for its ecoregions:

head(taxify_regions("Temperate Northern Atlantic", scheme = "meow"))
#>    code                           name            level2_name
#> 1 20030                   Adriatic Sea      Mediterranean Sea
#> 2 25033 Tunisian Plateau/Gulf of Sidra      Mediterranean Sea
#> 3 20021                  Faroe Plateau Northern European Seas
#> 4 25036                    Alboran Sea      Mediterranean Sea
#> 5 20025                      North Sea Northern European Seas
#> 6 20044                      Black Sea              Black Sea
#>                   level1_name scheme
#> 1 Temperate Northern Atlantic   meow
#> 2 Temperate Northern Atlantic   meow
#> 3 Temperate Northern Atlantic   meow
#> 4 Temperate Northern Atlantic   meow
#> 5 Temperate Northern Atlantic   meow
#> 6 Temperate Northern Atlantic   meow

Searching the realm name returns its ecoregions, with the province in level2_name, so region = "Temperate Northern Atlantic" covers all of them. A point at sea is placed in the MEOW ecoregion that contains it, as well as in a WGSRPD region on land, so one coords argument serves a list of plants and marine animals. MEOW covers coastal and shelf waters; a point over the deep ocean lies in no ecoregion and leaves marine names unconstrained. A WoRMS distribution record is as precise as the locality it was recorded against, which ranges from a bay to an ocean basin.

Choosing and combining backbones

This article shows how taxify decides which backbone resolves each name, how to name the backbones yourself, how to compare their answers for the same names, and how to download and pin backbone versions. It ends with a list of backbone choices for common kinds of name lists. Installation is covered in Get started.

  1. Match with the default fallback chain, or name the backbones in backbone.

  2. Compare the backbones’ answers with mode = "wide" or mode = "agreement".

  3. Check names a backbone does not cover with lookup_genus() and taxify_register_coverage().

  4. Download and pin versions with taxify_download(), and list them with list_backbones().

library(taxify)

The default fallback chain

A backbone is a published taxonomic database stored as a file on your disk. taxify supports 19 backbones of taxa, and two of vegetation units described in Matching vegetation units. list_backbones() reports each one’s scope, version, number of names and whether it is installed:

Without a backbone argument, taxify() uses every installed backbone of taxa as a fallback chain: each name is matched against the first backbone, the names left unmatched go to the second, and so on. The order is fixed by priority. The Extended Release of the Catalogue of Life (COL XR) comes first, then the Catalogue of Life (COL), then the backbones of one group (marine species, plants, fungi, algae, fishes, reptiles, mammals, birds, prokaryotes), then the broad compilations GBIF, ITIS, NCBI and OTT. On a new setup install_backbones() installs COL, GBIF and the Integrated Taxonomic Information System (ITIS); every backbone downloaded later joins the chain at its place in that order.

The list below mixes a plant, two animals renamed since their description, a fungus, a fish, a bacterium and a brown alga:

mixed <- c(
  "Quercus robur", "Panthera leo", "Amanita muscaria", "Salmo trutta",
  "Escherichia coli", "Macropus rufus", "Fucus vesiculosus",
  "Parus caeruleus"
)
res <- taxify(mixed, verbose = FALSE)
res[, c("input_name", "accepted_name", "backbone")]
#>          input_name       accepted_name backbone
#> 1     Quercus robur       Quercus robur    colxr
#> 2      Panthera leo        Panthera leo    colxr
#> 3  Amanita muscaria    Amanita muscaria    colxr
#> 4      Salmo trutta        Salmo trutta    colxr
#> 5  Escherichia coli    Escherichia coli    colxr
#> 6    Macropus rufus    Osphranter rufus    colxr
#> 7 Fucus vesiculosus   Fucus vesiculosus    colxr
#> 8   Parus caeruleus Cyanistes caeruleus    colxr
#> Sources: COLXR 2026.08 | cite() for full citations

All 19 backbones are installed on the machine that built this page, so the chain starts with COL XR. It resolves all eight names, and the other 18 backbones are skipped. The backbone column records which backbone matched each row, and the line under the table names the backbones to cite for this result: only those that matched a row. Macropus rufus and Parus caeruleus are synonyms in COL XR, and their rows carry its accepted names Osphranter rufus and Cyanistes caeruleus.

The first backbone that matches a name decides its accepted name. taxify does not take a vote across backbones: several backbones copy their treatment from one another, so a majority can follow the oldest treatment. Comparing backbones shows how to see where they disagree.

options(taxify.backbone_priority = ) changes the order for a session, and options(taxify.default_backbones = ) the set install_backbones() installs when called without arguments.

Naming the backbones

When most names of a list belong to one group, that group’s backbone can come first. A backbone vector replaces the default chain with your own; here the mixed list is resolved against World Flora Online (WFO) first, with COL for the rest. Before a name moves on, taxify checks the genus register, an index of the genera of every backbone, and does not send a name to a backbone that holds none of its genus:

res <- taxify(mixed, backbone = c("wfo", "col"))
#> Matching 8 names against 2 backbones: wfo -> col
#>   [wfo] Matching 8 names...
#>   [col] Matching 6 remaining names...
res[, c("input_name", "accepted_name", "match_type", "backbone")]
#>          input_name       accepted_name   match_type backbone
#> 1     Quercus robur       Quercus robur        exact      wfo
#> 2      Panthera leo        Panthera leo        exact      col
#> 3  Amanita muscaria    Amanita muscaria        exact      col
#> 4      Salmo trutta        Salmo trutta        exact      col
#> 5  Escherichia coli    Escherichia coli        exact      col
#> 6    Macropus rufus    Osphranter rufus        exact      col
#> 7 Fucus vesiculosus                <NA> out_of_scope     <NA>
#> 8   Parus caeruleus Cyanistes caeruleus        exact      col
#> Sources: WFO + COL | cite() for full citations

The progress lines show the chain: WFO matched Quercus robur, and COL received six of the seven remaining names. A name present in both backbones takes the treatment of the one listed first, including its accepted name, family and synonym resolution. Fuzzy matching runs inside each backbone before the name moves on, so a misspelt plant name is compared with WFO’s names before it reaches COL.

Fucus vesiculosus was not sent to COL, because the register records that neither WFO nor COL holds its genus; the row has match_type = "out_of_scope". The register is described in How names are matched. Adding AlgaeBase, the backbone for algae, resolves it:

res <- taxify(mixed, backbone = c("wfo", "algaebase", "col"),
              verbose = FALSE)
res[res$input_name == "Fucus vesiculosus",
    c("input_name", "accepted_name", "match_type", "backbone")]
#>          input_name     accepted_name match_type  backbone
#> 7 Fucus vesiculosus Fucus vesiculosus      exact algaebase
#> Sources: ALGAEBASE 2026.09 | cite() for full citations

Checking names outside a backbone

A row that comes back out_of_scope needs a backbone that holds its genus. lookup_genus() returns the genus register’s entry for a genus, and taxify_register_coverage() lists the backbones that hold it:

lookup_genus("Fucus")[, c("genus", "kingdom", "family", "life_form")]
#>        genus   kingdom   family  life_form
#> 176828 Fucus Chromista Fucaceae brown alga
taxify_register_coverage("Fucus")
#>   genus    backbone version date_added
#> 1 Fucus       worms 2026.09 2026-09-14
#> 2 Fucus sealifebase 2026.08 2026-08-27
#> 3 Fucus         ott 2026.09 2026-09-14
#> 4 Fucus        ncbi 2026.09 2026-09-14
#> 5 Fucus        itis 2026.09 2026-09-14
#> 6 Fucus        gbif 2023.08 2026-09-22
#> 7 Fucus       colxr 2026.08 2026-09-23
#> 8 Fucus   algaebase 2026.09 2026-09-14

The COL release used here is not among them, while COL XR, which adds names from further sources, is. An out_of_scope row therefore points to a backbone to add to the chain, where match_type = "none" leaves open whether the name is misspelt or exists in no backbone.

Comparing backbones

Backbones can treat the same name differently, and the fallback chain shows only the first one’s answer. mode = "wide" matches every name against every backbone in backbone and adds one accepted_<backbone> column per backbone, plus all_agree:

names3 <- c("Macropus rufus", "Parus caeruleus", "Quercus robur")
wide <- taxify(names3, backbone = c("col", "gbif", "itis"),
               mode = "wide", verbose = FALSE)
wide[, c("input_name", "accepted_col", "accepted_gbif", "accepted_itis",
         "all_agree")]
#>        input_name        accepted_col       accepted_gbif       accepted_itis
#> 1  Macropus rufus    Osphranter rufus      Macropus rufus    Osphranter rufus
#> 2 Parus caeruleus Cyanistes caeruleus Cyanistes caeruleus Cyanistes caeruleus
#> 3   Quercus robur       Quercus robur       Quercus robur       Quercus robur
#>   all_agree
#> 1     FALSE
#> 2      TRUE
#> 3      TRUE
#> Sources: COL + GBIF + ITIS | cite() for full citations

COL and ITIS place the red kangaroo in Osphranter; the GBIF Backbone Taxonomy keeps it in Macropus. accepted_name is still the pick of the fallback chain, here COL’s, so the result passes to the add_*() functions unchanged.

mode = "agreement" reports the same comparison as counts:

agr <- taxify(names3, backbone = c("col", "gbif", "itis"),
              mode = "agreement", verbose = FALSE)
agr[, c("input_name", "n_backbones_matched", "n_distinct_accepted",
        "all_agree")]
#>        input_name n_backbones_matched n_distinct_accepted all_agree
#> 1  Macropus rufus                   3                   2     FALSE
#> 2 Parus caeruleus                   3                   1      TRUE
#> 3   Quercus robur                   3                   1      TRUE
#> Sources: COL + GBIF + ITIS | cite() for full citations

all_agree is NA when fewer than two backbones matched a name.

Columns from one backbone

The output columns are the same for every backbone. Three functions add the columns that only one backbone carries, and fill them on the rows that backbone matched; other rows get NA:

  • add_wfo_info(): the place of publication, the parent name and WFO’s higher classification.

  • add_col_info(): COL’s classification from kingdom to order, nomenclatural code and status, and whether the species is extinct, marine, freshwater or terrestrial.

  • add_gbif_info(): the hybrid type, nomenclatural status, basionym author and year, and how the name entered the GBIF backbone.

crab <- taxify(c("Carcinus maenas", "Quercus robur"), backbone = "col",
               verbose = FALSE)
crab <- add_col_info(crab)
crab[, c("input_name", "kingdom", "is_marine", "is_terrestrial")]
#>        input_name  kingdom is_marine is_terrestrial
#> 1 Carcinus maenas Animalia      TRUE          FALSE
#> 2   Quercus robur  Plantae        NA             NA
#> Sources: COL 2026.09 | cite() for full citations

COL matched the oak as well but records no habitat for it, so NA there means not recorded, not unmatched.

How the backbones differ in output

The columns hold the same kind of value for every backbone, with these differences:

  • Identifiers. taxon_id and accepted_id use each backbone’s own system: WFO IDs, opaque COL IDs (4R5YN for Quercus robur), GBIF integer keys, ITIS TSNs, NCBI Taxonomy IDs, WoRMS AphiaIDs.

  • Authorship. NCBI and OTT record no authors, so authorship is NA on their rows.

  • Synonyms. Each source encodes synonyms in its own way.

An ID is meaningful only within its backbone. When a backbone is built, its synonyms are converted to is_synonym, accepted_name and accepted_id, and a synonym that points to another synonym is followed to the accepted name.

The source, licence and citation of each backbone are listed in The backbones and datasets.

Downloading and pinning versions

Each backbone is downloaded once into taxify_data_dir() and read from disk after that. install_backbones() and taxify_download() fetch backbones ahead of time, for a server or a session without network access. A function that needs a backbone that is not on disk asks before downloading it in an interactive session; in a script it stops with an error naming the install_backbones() call, unless options(taxify.auto_download = TRUE) or the environment variable TAXIFY_AUTO_DOWNLOAD allows the download:

taxify_download(c("wfo", "worms"))

The download goes into a latest folder that is replaced when a newer version is released. taxify compares the local versions with the published ones once per session, at the first call, and updates latest then; the version never changes during a session. The replaced version is deleted. Without network access, or with options(taxify.offline = TRUE) or the environment variable TAXIFY_OFFLINE set, it uses the files on disk.

taxify_store() lists the builds on disk with their sizes. taxify_clean() removes builds taxify no longer reads and files left by an interrupted download; taxify_clean("gbif") removes one backbone, and taxify_clean("all") the whole data directory.

A version string downloads into a folder of its own that is never replaced:

taxify_download("wfo", version = "2024.01")

Every row records the backbone version that matched it:

unique(res$backbone_version)
#> [1] "wfo:2026.06 (2026-09-15)"       "col:2026.09 (2026-09-14)"      
#> [3] "algaebase:2026.09 (2026-09-14)"

taxify_lock() writes these versions with a checksum of each file to a JSON file, and taxify_restore(install = TRUE) reinstalls the same builds on another machine, as shown in Get started. taxify_build() builds a backbone from its source files instead of downloading it, which needs the taxifydb package.

Choosing backbones for a list

The first backbone in the vector sets the treatment for most names, and broader backbones after it resolve the rest:

  • Vascular plants: "wfo", or c("wfo", "col") for cultivars, names from older floras and recently moved genera that WFO lacks. The World Checklist of Vascular Plants ("wcvp") and the Leipzig Catalogue of Vascular Plants ("lcvp") are alternatives with their own treatment.

  • European vegetation data: c("euromed", "wfo"). Euro+Med PlantBase covers the native and introduced vascular plants of Europe, the Mediterranean and the Caucasus, and WFO resolves the names outside it.

  • Marine and aquatic species: "worms", the World Register of Marine Species, with "col" after it for the terrestrial taxa of an estuarine list.

  • Fungi: c("fungorum", "col"), Species Fungorum first.

  • Algae: c("algaebase", "col"). AlgaeBase is licensed for non-commercial use only (CC BY-NC).

  • Fishes, reptiles, mammals, birds, prokaryotes: "fishbase", "reptiledb", "mdd", "avilist" or "lpsn", each followed by "col".

  • Sequence-linked lists (metabarcoding, eDNA): c("ncbi", "col"). NCBI Taxonomy IDs link to GenBank records, and NCBI covers bacteria, archaea and viruses.

  • Phylogenetic work: "ott", the Open Tree of Life taxonomy, which cross-references NCBI, GBIF, WoRMS and other sources.

  • Lists of unknown composition: the default chain, or c("col", "gbif").
